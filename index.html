PARMCO

An iPhone interface for controlling a stepper motor over Bluetooth, with independent measurements of speed and shaft position.

Explore the system

System

From the phone to the motor

The iPhone sends commands over Bluetooth Low Energy. The Raspberry Pi interprets them and drives the stepper motor through the SN754410NE driver. The 4N25 provides the driver’s enable signal.

iPhone app

Control, measure, and test

The SwiftUI app connects to the Pi over Bluetooth Low Energy. Its controls set direction, speed, and operating mode, start and stop the motor, calibrate zero, and request an angle target. Live status reports IR RPM, encoder RPM, and shaft angle.

Guided tests

Choose Test A–G and its settings from the phone. The Pi runs the sequence and sends progress, countdowns, and prompts back to the app. A stop control cancels the test and stops the motor.

Independent readings

Enter blade counts and protractor readings when prompted. These observations provide references for checking the sensor measurements.

Results on the phone

Retrieve saved runs and test summaries from the Pi. The app displays the returned tables and graphs using Swift Charts, with recovery for missing result packets during Bluetooth transfer.

Final validation

Measurements behind the motion

Final data collection is in progress. We compare recorded sensor values with independent blade counts and protractor readings, then evaluate repeatability and measurement limits across repeated runs.

Tests used to evaluate the final system
TestWhat we measure
A · RPM agreementCommanded, IR, encoder, and independent RPM across the speed range.
B · Missed stepsActual shaft position after an open-loop run that loses steps.
C · Angle accuracyTarget error, repeatability, and the effect of approach direction.
D · Step responseRise time, settling time, overshoot, and final angle error.
E · Disturbance recoverySpeed recovery after a disturbance in Manual and Maintain modes.
F · Feedback holdPosition recovery after nudges, holding behavior, and supply current.
G · Return to zeroRecovery of the calibrated zero after the motor loses steps.
